Automated Latin Manuscript Classification
❧ ✦ ☙
H

istorical paleography is the science of deciphering, dating, and localizing handwritten scriptoria. This project addresses the challenge of automatic manuscript categorization with traditional archival scholarship by using an ensemble deep learning architecture combining ConvNeXt-Small and EfficientNet-V2-S (384x384 resolution) trained on the benchmark ICDAR CLaMM (Classification of Latin Medieval Manuscripts) dataset to classify historical handwriting into 12 distinct paleographic script types of the dataset. The model learns subtle ductus, pen stroke angles, ligature morphologies, and minim structures to classify Latin manuscript pages into twelve distinct paleographical traditions.

By applying weighted probability averaging (60% ConvNeXt / 40% EfficientNet), the system captures both fine-grained stroke morphology and macroscopic spatial text layouts. This multi-architecture approach eliminates single-model variance and overcomes complex visual overlaps inherent to historical manuscripts, achieving a peak validation accuracy of 80.40% (F₁= 0.81) to deliver reliable automated manuscript categorization.

Paleographical Characteristics & Ductus

Standardized under Charlemagne and Alcuin of York to unify imperial literacy. Noted for round, open forms, clear word spacing, minimal abbreviations, and uniform ascenders with slight clubbing or serif finials. Replaced regional scripts (Merovingian, Beneventan, Visigothic) across mainland Europe.
